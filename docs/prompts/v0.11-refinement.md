## 1. No visible pop-in, anywhere (top priority)

**Goal: the player never sees scenery appear, disappear or change shape.** No trees, rocks,
grass or flowers popping in, swapping, or fading in front of them during normal play. Earlier
versions (v0.6.1 and before) had no noticeable pop-in; v0.10 does, most visibly in the upper
forest toward treeline on my iPhone (my screenshots: the same spot a few seconds apart, with the
ridge trees looking different). If it can still be noticed, this item isn't done.

**Why it started (from the code):**
- v0.6.1 swapped trees to their simpler far version at **150 m**. Since v0.7 the swap is at
  **55 m**, close enough to see.
- v0.8–v0.9 cut ground-cover draw distances to **32–64 m** (they were **110–260 m**), so
  grass and flowers appear just ahead of the climber.
- Swaps happen a whole chunk (up to ~230 m of trees) at a time, so many things change at once.
- The far whitebark pine is a single "lollipop" clump; the near one has several, so it visibly
  changes shape.

**What to do:**
- **Move every change out of sight.** Swap trees to their far version, and fade out ground
  cover, only at distances where the difference can't be seen on a phone screen (at least as far
  as v0.6.1: trees 150 m or more, ground cover 110 m or more), behind fog where possible. Where a
  near and far version would still look different at that range, keep the near version.
- **Make far versions match their near versions** (height, width, crown shape, colour), so a
  swap that does happen is invisible. Check every tree and rock pair side by side in the kit
  preview. Fix the whitebark pine first.
- **No chunk-wide changes.** Swap per tree by distance (or with much smaller chunks), with a
  slow cross-fade (about 1–1.5 s) as a safety net, not as the main fix.
- **Grass and flowers far away:** where cover would be too costly to draw at range, put a subtle
  grass/flower tint on the terrain so the ground looks the same before and after cover appears.
- **Pay for it elsewhere if needed.** Visual stability comes before the v0.6.1 triangle budget.
  If the longer distances cost too much, reduce detail somewhere the player can't notice
  (density far away, simpler near-versions of grass), not by bringing the swap closer. Report
  the performance cost.
- **Test it like a player:** walk the whole trail on a phone-sized screen at phone frame rates
  (30 fps), especially the upper forest and treeline, and compare frames a second or two apart.
  Report any place where something still appears or changes.

## 2. A real treeline

"Treeline reached" shows at about 6,830 ft, but there are still about 1,400 trees above that
height (about 1,000 above 7,000 ft), so the message feels wrong.

- Thin the forest out quickly toward the treeline: above it, only sparse, stunted trees
  (low wind-shaped whitebark pine and subalpine fir krummholz, in small clumps in sheltered
  hollows), dying out completely a few hundred feet higher.
- Move the "Treeline" checkpoint, or the tree bands, so the two agree. The point where the
  message appears should be where the forest visibly ends.
- Keep the meadows, heather and alpine grass above treeline.
- Check the effect on performance (fewer trees should help) and on the look of the tarn area.

## 3. Score screen: collapsed by default, cleaner data

The full score panel covers most of the summit view.

- **Collapsed by default.** Show only the top: score, real time, "new best" and the rank title
  (e.g. "Steady Mountaineer"), plus a clear "Show details" control. Tap/click expands to the
  full breakdown and climb stats; collapse again with the same control. Keep all the data.
- Keep the collapsed panel small and low, so the summit view is visible above it.

## 4. Rethink the score breakdown and its visuals

The bars are repetitive and don't fit every item. "Clean climb" and "Register signed" are
yes/no, and "Summit reached" is almost always 500, so a bar makes little sense.

Propose and build a clearer system, keeping **finish time as the main factor**:

- **Time** stays the core score. Show it against meaningful markers rather than a bar: your
  time, your personal best, and a par/target time (e.g. a small timeline with ticks).
- **Gems**: show the 11 gems as icons, filled for the ones collected (green and red), instead of
  a bar. Gems **stay as bonus points** for now (50 each, as today), shown next to the icons.
- **Yes/no items** (clean climb, register signed, all gems, stayed on trail) become badges or
  achievement chips (earned / not earned), with any bonus shown next to them, not bars.
- **Summit reached**: drop it as a score line. A finished climb is the requirement for a score.
  For a climb that doesn't reach the summit, record it in the expedition log as an attempt with
  its high point and time, with no score on the board.
- Ideas for other variables that take time "a step further" (e.g. pacing, resources left,
  time on trail) go in the report with their trade-offs. Don't build them this round.
- Leaderboards and personal bests must stay comparable: if the formula changes, give the new
  score a new version so old and new scores aren't mixed on the same board.
- Show the formula clearly in the details (e.g. "Time 11,844 + gems 200 + badges 200 =
  12,244").
