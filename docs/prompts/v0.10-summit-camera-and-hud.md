## 1. Summit celebration: the pull-back reveal

At the summit, the camera should show the scale of the climb: a tiny climber on top of a big
mountain, with the surrounding ranges laid out around it. Replace the current celebration
orbit with a "drone pull-back" in three beats, about 14 seconds, then a slow orbit:

1. **Close (0–2 s).** Near eye level, just behind the climber's shoulder, looking out at the
   next range. The climber fills about a third of the frame.
2. **Pull-back (2–10 s).** The camera flies backward and upward along a smooth curve, toward
   the side the trail came up, so the summit, snowfield, cinder switchbacks and forest fall
   away below.
   - Height rises faster than distance at first (a short, steep lift), then distance takes
     over. The camera ends about 150–250 m out, roughly level with or slightly above the
     summit, so the distant peaks stand above the skyline (in perspective, not top-down).
   - The camera tilts down gently as it rises, keeping the summit about a third of the way
     down the frame.
   - The lens widens slightly (about 55° → 70°) during the move, so the climber shrinks
     faster than the mountain does: that sells the scale.
   - Ease in and out (no sudden starts or stops).
3. **Hold and orbit (10 s onward).** A slow orbit at that distance while the light changes, so
   the far peaks, the crater and the route all pass through the frame. Drag and pinch still
   take over, as now.

Details:
- **Keep the climber visible** all the way through. Stop the pull-back at the distance where
  the climber is still clearly readable, not a lost pixel: small against the mountain, but
  you can see them. Add a subtle highlight or soft marker ring only if needed.
- **Explore the view afterwards.** Once the reveal ends, the player can orbit (drag/arrow
  keys) and zoom in and out (pinch/scroll/+−) freely: in to the climber at the cairn, out to
  the wide view. Zoom range from close-up to about the reveal distance. Recenter (C) returns
  to the reveal framing.
- The change is mainly to the opening of the celebration: the first seconds show the scale
  and the surrounding landscape.
- The camera never dips into terrain and rises over nearby ridges.
- Fog, mist and the near plane adapt so distant ranges stay crisp during the pull-back.
- On a phone in portrait, the pull-back goes less far and sits a little higher, so the whole
  mountain fits.
- Respect reduced motion: a shorter, gentler move.
- Keep the score panel from covering the climber at the key moment (time the panel so the
  pull-back plays first, or keep the climber in the open part of the frame).

## 2. Cleaner HUD on desktop

The key hints panel (WASD move · Shift dash · …) and the bottom bar take up a lot of room on
desktop.

- **Hide the key hints.** Add a small "hide" control on the hints panel. Once hidden, it stays
  hidden next time (remember the choice on this device).
- **Bring them back quickly.** A small, unobtrusive "Controls" button (a keyboard icon) in the
  corner, plus a key (`H` or `?`), shows the hints again. Also add the option to the pause menu.
- **Move the bottom bar lower** to open up the view: the energy and water bars and the
  drink/eat/rest buttons sit closer to the bottom edge, more compact, still clear of the
  screen edge and safe areas.
- **Quick actions menu.** One small button (and a key) opens a compact menu with the
  less-used actions: show controls, map, recenter camera, pause. That way the main view only
  needs the essentials.
- Phones: keep the current touch layout unless something clearly benefits from the same
  change. Check it still fits on an iPhone 16 Pro.

## 3. General bug sweep

The gameplay feels close to refined. Do a focused pass for bugs, without redesigning anything:
- Read through the main flows (start → climb → summit → score → restart, pause/resume,
  map, signs, register, gems, drink/eat/rest, boundary) and fix anything broken or
  inconsistent.
- Desktop and phone layouts: overlapping panels, clipped text, buttons off-screen, safe areas.
- Console errors and warnings during a run (including the deprecated `THREE.Clock` warning).
- Saved data: settings and records survive a reload; a corrupt or missing save doesn't break
  the game.
- Report each bug found and fixed in one line; list anything found but left alone, and why.
