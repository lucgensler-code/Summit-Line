## A. Bugs and performance (Chromebook first)

1. **Strobing white outlines around rocks, trees and grass (Chromebook).** Edges flash and blink
   like a strobe. It happens everywhere, while walking and while standing still, and it's
   worst on the summit screen, where every tree down in the valley strobes. Find the cause
   before fixing anything. Because it also happens standing still and on far-away trees, look
   first at causes that don't need movement. Leads from the code:
   - **Depth precision:** distant trees, trunks and terrain fighting for the same depth on the
     Chromebook GPU. Check the camera's near/far planes during play and on the summit screen;
     consider a larger near plane or a logarithmic depth buffer.
   - **Near/far swap with no margin:** a chunk sitting right at the swap distance can flip
     between versions every frame from tiny camera movement (idle bob, summit orbit). Add a
     margin (hysteresis) so it only switches once.
   - The sun's shadow camera follows the player but isn't snapped to the shadow-map texel grid.
     That makes shadow edges shimmer and crawl as you move. Snap it to whole texels, and check
     shadow bias and normal bias on the flat-shaded and double-sided plant materials.
   - Post-processing (bloom + grade) renders into a 4× multisampled half-float target. Some
     Chromebook GPUs don't support that well, which can drop anti-aliasing or make bright edges
     sparkle. Check what the device actually gets, and fall back to an 8-bit target or turn off
     bloom where needed.
   - Near and far detail versions swap per chunk; check for flicker at the swap distance.
   - Add a `?debug` overlay (hidden by default) with live toggles for post-processing, bloom,
     shadows, mist and pixel ratio, plus fps and the current quality tier, so I can find the
     culprit on the Chromebook myself.
2. **Multicoloured dots on clouds**, mostly at higher altitude and on mobile. Likely
   precision/bloom artefacts on the cloud sprites, stars drawing through clouds, or noise in the
   colour grade. Find the cause and fix it, and check at sunrise, midday, sunset and night.
3. **Compass north looks buggy.** On the full map (M), the north badge draws its arrowhead
   (y −13…−7) on top of the "N" letter (baseline y −2), so the two merge into a house-like
   glyph. The badge also sits right against the map's edge. Redraw it cleanly: arrow above,
   "N" centred below, nothing overlapping, inset from the edge. Check the HUD compass and the
   minimap N marker the same way.
3b. **Square particles.** Drifting dust and snow motes show up as small grey squares, in front
   of trees and in the night sky. Make them soft round dots, sized for the screen's pixel
   density, and fewer on phones.
4. **Performance budget.** Get v0.7's meadow (+13% triangles) and sunrise (+23%) views back to the
   v0.6.1 budget. Aim for smooth play on the Chromebook (state the fps target you pick) and an
   iPhone 16 Pro. Report draw calls, triangles and frame time per view vs v0.6.1 and v0.7.
5. **Tighter play area** (also helps performance). Today the boundary is a circle around the
   whole mountain, so you can get 2+ miles off the trail. Replace it with a corridor of
   **0.5 mi** either side of the trail, measured to the nearest point of the main trail or a
   marked spur.
   - Show the edge naturally before it stops you: denser trees, cliffs or a boundary sign,
     plus the existing toast. Make the stop soft (slow down, then turn back), not an invisible
     wall.
   - Don't generate near-detail scenery outside the corridor. Keep distant forest and terrain
     as backdrop.
   - Keep every gem, the lake and shortcuts inside the corridor. Design it so later versions
     can add longer shortcuts.

## B. Visual fixes I called out

6. **Trees: more distinct species and higher fidelity.** Fir and hemlock look alike right now.
   - Douglas fir: tall, straight, conical, with regular, slightly upturned tiers and a
     darker blue-green. Western/mountain hemlock: softer, drooping, layered tiers, a finer
     yellower green, and a nodding top. Subalpine fir: a narrow spire with a dark blue-grey
     green. Whitebark pine: wind-shaped, multi-stem, rounded clumps, yellow-green.
   - Make the differences show in silhouette, colour and texture (tier count, droop,
     raggedness, branch-tip clumps). Add at least one more variation per species: a younger
     or slimmer form, a broken top, a dense or sparse crown.
   - **Bent tops:** use them on fewer trees (hemlocks only, about 30%), and have them all lean
     roughly the same way (downwind, with a small random spread).
   - Raise near detail where the budget allows (more irregular tier edges, branch tufts),
     paid for by the far version.
7. **Distant trees look low-fidelity and have a thin point or line at the top.** The
   screenshots show long thin spikes on far trees and night silhouettes. Cause from the code:
   the far version stretches its 2 tiers (taller, and widest at the tip), then applies the
   bent-top offset to that taller apex, which turns into a long bent needle. Fix the far
   version's apex (no needle spikes); far trees should keep a natural, slightly rounded tip,
   matching the near tree's silhouette. Consider billboard impostors (flat picture cards of the
   trees) for the farthest forest: better silhouettes at lower cost.
8. **Distant mountains: natural snow caps.** v0.7's changes didn't show on screen, and the
   peaks still read as all white. Give them caps that follow the ridges and gullies: a broken
   snowline, rock ribs and dark faces showing through, steep faces bare, with the amount
   varying by peak. Leave a "fully glaciated" setting for future mountains. Confirm the change
   is visible in screenshots at all times of day.
9. **Cairns look precariously balanced; some stones don't touch.** Rest each stone on the
   real top surface of the one below (use the actual mesh bounds, not an estimate). Make the
   stones flatter and wider at the base, with slight offsets only, and no gaps or floating
   stones. Seat the cairn into the ground on slopes.
9b. **Summit**
    - Remove the rock inside the crater (the pale boxy rock left of the summit cairn). Check
      the remaining summit rocks for odd shapes.
    - The summit cairn reads as a pile of dark cubes. Rebuild it as a proper large cairn with
      the same stone rules as item 9, lighter grey-brown with lichen.
    - The sunset summit view is very hazy and pink, and the distant peak behind it has no
      readable snow cap or rock. Tone the haze down so the view rewards the climb, together
      with item 8.
10. **Home screen**
    - Remove the rocks from the mountain preview.
    - Fix the gap on the right side of the trail preview line.
11. **Start camera lower**, closer to the climber's eye level (I drop it almost immediately
    every time). Keep the player's own camera adjustments as they are.
12. **Lake (Obsidian Tarn): a side spur and a sign**, so it's worth visiting. The lake stays
    where it is. Add a short marked spur trail from the main trail to the shore, with a trail
    sign at the junction ("Obsidian Tarn ↗"). The main route, its timing and gem positions stay
    the same. The spur counts as on-trail. Re-run the ascent sim.
13. **Summit score: time bar in red**, to set it apart from the primary orange.
